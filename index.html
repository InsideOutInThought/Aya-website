The origins of Aya

Rooted in Ghanaian heritage. Inspired by the fern.

Aya means fern. To the Akan people of Ghana, it is an Adinkra symbol of endurance and resourcefulness, a plant that grows in hard places.

Our mission

A place for witnessed transformation.

Aya Recovery & Wellness provides a safe home where each woman in substance use recovery moves through eight levels of witnessed transformation across her health, family, work, and purpose. She returns to community with roots and wings.

Our vision

A world where recovery is met with belonging, transformation is supported by community, and every person has the freedom to build a life of purpose.

Our philosophy

We grow as individuals, we thrive in community, and we share responsibility for the conditions that make both possible.

Need help before we open?

Roots & Wings opens in Fall 2027. If you or someone you love needs support now, you don't have to wait for us.

  • In an emergency or a possible overdoseCall 911.
  • In crisisCall or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time. That includes a substance use crisis.
  • A recovery residence open nowSearch FARR's directory of certified housing across Florida.
  • Domestic violenceCall Florida's Domestic Violence Hotline, 1-800-500-1119, any time, in English, Spanish, or Creole. An advocate can help you plan for safety and connect you with a certified center near you. You can also text START to 88788 for the National Domestic Violence Hotline.
  • Sexual assaultCall the National Sexual Assault Hotline, 1-800-656-4673, or text HOPE to 64673, any time. It's free and confidential, in English and Spanish. Florida's helpline, 1-888-956-7273, can connect you with a center close to home.
  • Human traffickingCall the National Human Trafficking Hotline, 1-888-373-7888, or text 233733. Advocates answer 24/7.
  • A safe place to stayDial 211 to find emergency shelter and other help near you.
  • Treatment and support near youSAMHSA's National Helpline, 1-800-662-4357, is free and open 24/7 in English and Spanish. You can also search FindTreatment.gov.
